Citation graph — five visualization options

Same corpus: 12 papers, ~25 citation edges. Real authors, real years, real rebuttals. Pick the structure that reads the argument most cleanly. Sunfire palette throughout.

Option 1

Force-directed graph

Physics simulation positions nodes. Clusters emerge from citation density. Hub-and-spoke shape reveals which papers are load-bearing without saying so. Nodes sized by citation count; edges weighted by influence.

Option 2

Radial cascade from the seed paper

de Vries 2018 at the origin, citation lines fanning right. Downstream papers ordered by year, the cascade visible as a spreading beam. This is the Litmaps gesture — clean, immediate, makes one paper visibly load-bearing.

Option 3

Arc diagram along a time baseline

Papers on a horizontal time axis (2008 → 2026), arcs above connect citing→cited pairs. Arc thickness = citation count of the source. Rebuttals drop below the line. Reveals the temporal cascade and shows visually how recent the rebuttals are vs how long the original claims have stood.

Option 4

Bipartite — primary measurement vs derivative

Two columns: PRIMARY DATA on the left (CBECI, BECI, Stoll, Krause — anything with actual measurement), DERIVATIVE WORK on the right (Mora, Truby, Howson — extrapolation, commentary, policy review). Edges show which downstream paper draws from which primary source. Makes the concentration visible: nearly every edge points back to two or three nodes.

Option 5

Constellation map — methodology clusters

Papers placed in 2D by methodology type: empirical bottom-up (right), commentary (top-left), policy review (bottom-left), institutional model (centre). Edges only for direct citations. Star-map aesthetic; readers see at a glance that the "evidence" cluster contains two papers and the "commentary / policy" cluster contains six.
